Kubernetes

Set requests, limits, and probes on every pod

Three one-time settings per workload that quietly prevent a large share of cluster incidents.

Requests: let the scheduler do its job

Resource requests tell Kubernetes how much a pod actually needs so it can place it sensibly. Without them the scheduler guesses, packs nodes wrong, and you get noisy-neighbour stalls that look like random slowness.

Limits: contain the blast radius

Limits stop one misbehaving pod from eating a node’s memory or CPU and taking its neighbours down with it. Set them deliberately — too low and you get throttling and OOMKills, too high and limits stop meaning anything.

Probes: don’t route traffic to the not-ready

Readiness probes keep traffic away from a container that hasn’t finished starting; liveness probes restart one that has wedged. Missing them is why deploys briefly serve errors even though ‘the pods are running’.

A one-time cost that pays every incident

Put all three in your base manifests or Helm chart so every new workload inherits them. It’s a few lines once, and it removes a whole category of 2 a.m. pages.
