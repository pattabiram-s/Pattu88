CI/CD

Make pipelines fail fast, loudly

A pipeline earns trust by telling developers what broke, within seconds — not ten minutes later.

Order stages by cost and failure rate

Run the cheap, high-signal checks first — format, lint, type-check, unit tests — then build, then the slow integration and end-to-end suites. Most breakages are caught in the first thirty seconds, so most pushes never pay for the slow stages at all.

Fail loudly and specifically

A red build should name the one thing that failed and link straight to the log line. Vague “pipeline failed” messages train people to click “re-run” instead of reading. Surface the failing test, file, and assertion in the status check itself.

Keep it fast enough to stay in flow

Past roughly ten minutes, developers context-switch and stop watching. Cache dependencies and layers, run independent jobs in parallel, and split the suite so the common path is quick. Protect that budget — a slow pipeline quietly becomes a pipeline people route around.

What ‘good’ looks like

Pull-request feedback in under five minutes, a single obvious reason for every failure, and flaky tests quarantined rather than tolerated. Green means shippable, and everyone believes it.
