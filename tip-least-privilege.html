Security

Least privilege is a default, not a cleanup task

Starting at zero access and adding only what a workload proves it needs is far cheaper than clawing permissions back later.

Start every identity at zero

Each IAM role, service account, security group, and database grant begins with no access. You add a permission only when something concrete fails without it. The diff that grants access becomes a readable record of exactly what the workload does.

Why retrofitting is so painful

Once a role is broad and live, nobody knows which of its permissions are load-bearing. Removing one risks a 2 a.m. page, so teams leave it — and broad, stale roles are precisely what turns a single compromised credential into a full breach.

Make it the easy path

Bake least-privilege roles into your Terraform modules and templates so the default new service is already scoped. Review access with the same seriousness as code. Prefer short-lived, assumed credentials over long-lived keys, and expire what isn’t used.

Signs you got it wrong

Wildcards in policies, a handful of admin roles shared across teams, and service accounts whose last-used permissions no one can explain. If you can’t say why an identity has a grant, it probably shouldn’t.
